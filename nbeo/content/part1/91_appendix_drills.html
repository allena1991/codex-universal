Mixed final drill

Fifty items sampling all seventeen condition areas. Give yourself 77 seconds each, which is the real exam pace, and log every error by condition area and discipline.

Questions 1 to 25 No looking back. Write your answers, then check the key below.
  1. Which wall of the orbit is thinnest, and which most commonly fractures in a blowout?
  2. A patient has a 1.5 mm ptosis, miosis, and anhidrosis of the whole ipsilateral body. Which order of neuron is affected?
  3. Trace lacrimal parasympathetic innervation from nucleus to gland.
  4. Which corneal layer never regenerates, and what does injury to it produce?
  5. Give the two collagen-related reasons the cornea is transparent and the sclera is not.
  6. Name the structure housing limbal stem cells and one disease that destroys it.
  7. Which trabecular layer creates most outflow resistance?
  8. Write the Goldmann equation and identify the term altered by a carotid-cavernous fistula.
  9. Explain how pilocarpine lowers IOP and what it does to uveoscleral outflow.
  10. Which epithelium forms the blood-aqueous barrier, and name its two enzymes.
  11. Anterior versus posterior lens capsule thickness, and the surgical significance.
  12. Superotemporal versus inferonasal ectopia lentis: disease and molecular defect.
  13. Rank the top three sites of vitreoretinal adhesion and the lesion each produces.
  14. Which retinal layer marks the boundary between retinal and choroidal blood supply?
  15. Name the two blood-retinal barriers and the cell type responsible for each.
  16. What is the earliest histologic lesion of diabetic retinopathy?
  17. Why does a glaucomatous arcuate defect stop abruptly at the horizontal midline nasally?
  18. Which ganglion cell class contains melanopsin and where do its axons project?
  19. Give the four segments of the optic nerve with approximate lengths.
  20. A pituitary adenoma first produces which field defect, and why that quadrant?
  21. Which LGN layers are magnocellular, and which receive crossed input?
  22. Define internuclear ophthalmoplegia and give the commonest cause in a 28-year-old.
  23. List the ciliary ganglion's three roots and state which synapses.
  24. Give the three-drug sequence for a suspected Horner syndrome and what each proves.
  25. Assign corneal epithelium, corneal stroma, iris stroma, and iris sphincter to embryonic sources.
Key 1 to 25
  1. Medial (lamina papyracea) is thinnest; the posteromedial floor fractures most commonly because it is unsupported over the maxillary sinus.
  2. First order (central), because whole-body anhidrosis implies a lesion above the sympathetic outflow.
  3. Lacrimal (superior salivatory) nucleus, CN VII via nervus intermedius, greater superficial petrosal nerve, nerve of the pterygoid canal (vidian), pterygopalatine ganglion, zygomatic branch of V2, then the lacrimal nerve of V1.
  4. Bowman layer; injury leaves permanent scarring.
  5. Uniform small fibril diameter (22 to 35 nm) and regular short spacing giving destructive interference; the sclera has irregular diameters (25 to 230 nm) and irregular spacing.
  6. Palisades of Vogt; chemical burn (also Stevens-Johnson, aniridia, contact lens overwear).
  7. Juxtacanalicular (cribriform) tissue with the inner wall of Schlemm canal.
  8. Po = (F − U)/C + Pv; a carotid-cavernous fistula raises Pv.
  9. Longitudinal ciliary muscle contraction pulls on the scleral spur, widening trabecular spaces and increasing conventional outflow; uveoscleral outflow decreases.
  10. Non-pigmented ciliary epithelium; Na+/K+ ATPase and carbonic anhydrase II.
  11. Anterior about 14 to 15.5 µm, posterior about 4 µm; the posterior capsule is the fragile structure at risk in phacoemulsification.
  12. Superotemporal: Marfan, FBN1 fibrillin-1. Inferonasal: homocystinuria, cystathionine β-synthase.
  13. Vitreous base (giant tear or dialysis), lattice margins (horseshoe tear), optic disc (Weiss ring on PVD).
  14. The outer plexiform layer.
  15. Inner: retinal capillary endothelial tight junctions with pericytes. Outer: RPE tight junctions.
  16. Pericyte loss.
  17. Arcuate nerve fibre bundles arch around the macula and do not cross the horizontal temporal raphe.
  18. Intrinsically photosensitive retinal ganglion cells; to the suprachiasmatic nucleus and the olivary pretectal nucleus.
  19. Intraocular 1 mm, intraorbital 25 to 30 mm, intracanalicular 4 to 10 mm, intracranial 10 to 16 mm.
  20. Bitemporal superior quadrantanopia, because upward growth compresses the inferior chiasmal fibres, which carry the superior field.
  21. Layers 1 and 2 are magnocellular; crossed fibres go to layers 1, 4, and 6.
  22. A medial longitudinal fasciculus lesion causing ipsilateral adduction failure with contralateral abducting nystagmus; multiple sclerosis, especially if bilateral.
  23. Parasympathetic (synapses), sensory from nasociliary (passes through), sympathetic from the carotid plexus (passes through).
  24. Cocaine or apraclonidine confirms a Horner syndrome at any level; hydroxyamphetamine then localises, dilating in first- and second-order lesions and failing in third-order.
  25. Corneal epithelium: surface ectoderm. Corneal stroma: neural crest. Iris stroma: neural crest. Iris sphincter: neuroectoderm.

Key 26 to 50
  1. L′ = L + F. L = 1/(−0.20) = −5.00 D; L′ = −5 + 10 = +5.00 D, so the image is 20 cm to the right, real and inverted, magnification −1.
  2. Fnew = −12 / (1 − 0.012 × −12) = −12/1.144 = −10.49, so about −10.50 D.
  3. +1.50 +2.50 × 020.
  4. θ from the cylinder axis (090) to 180 is 90°, so F = −2.00 + (−2.00)(sin290) = −4.00 D.
  5. OC is 11 mm below the top, so jump = 1.1 × 3.00 = 3.30 Δ.
  6. Polycarbonate: best impact resistance, Abbe about 30.
  7. At least 24 for daily wear and 87 for extended wear, targeting no more than about 4% oedema.
  8. Fitted 0.50 D steeper, giving a +0.50 D tear lens; SAM, so add 0.50 D of minus to the lens power.
  9. 300/20 = +15.00 D, implying a working distance of about 6.7 cm.
  10. The spectacle-mounted high-plus microscope; the cost is a very short working distance and the need for base-in prism or monocular correction.
  11. AC/A = 6.2 + 0.4(14 − 2) = 6.2 + 4.8 = 11:1: a high AC/A with near esophoria, so convergence excess, treated with a plus add.
  12. Right superior oblique.
  13. Two hours daily for moderate amblyopia, six hours for severe.
  14. Outer retinal disease gives blue-yellow, optic nerve disease gives red-green. Exceptions: glaucoma and dominant optic atrophy (optic nerve but blue-yellow), Stargardt and cone dystrophy (outer retina but red-green).
  15. The Hardy-Rand-Rittler (HRR) plates.
  16. The b-wave, recorded through Müller cell potassium currents; a normal a-wave with a reduced b-wave (negative ERG) localises to the inner retina: congenital stationary night blindness, X-linked retinoschisis, CRAO, quinine toxicity.
  17. Best vitelliform macular dystrophy; BEST1 (bestrophin).
  18. Demyelination, as in optic neuritis and multiple sclerosis.
  19. Neisseria gonorrhoeae and meningitidis, Corynebacterium diphtheriae, Shigella, Haemophilus (aegyptius / influenzae biotype III), and Listeria; Pseudomonas invades rapidly with a lens biofilm.
  20. No lymphatics or vessels in cornea, lens, and vitreous; blood-ocular barriers; immunosuppressive aqueous (TGF-β2 and neuropeptides); Fas ligand expression; low MHC class II with few central antigen-presenting cells; and ACAID.
  21. c-ANCA / anti-PR3; necrotising scleritis and an orbital inflammatory mass with bone destruction.
  22. Severe haemorrhages in 4 quadrants, or venous beading in 2, or IRMA in 1 defines severe NPDR; review every 3 to 4 months. High-risk PDR is NVD greater than one third of a disc area, any NVD with vitreous or preretinal haemorrhage, or NVE greater than half a disc area with haemorrhage.
  23. Loteprednol etabonate, because it is an ester steroid hydrolysed to inactive metabolites within the eye.
  24. Above 5.0 mg/kg/day of real body weight; annual screening after five years (baseline within a year); automated visual fields plus SD-OCT.
  25. ARR = 5%; RR = 10/15 = 0.67; NNT = 1/0.05 = 20.
Final checklist before you sit the exam
  1. Systemic health is 19% of the exam. Have you actually revised it, or only the ocular anatomy you enjoy?
  2. Ametropia plus ophthalmic optics is 14%. Can you do vertex, transposition, oblique meridian, and prism calculations under time pressure without a calculator?
  3. Conjunctiva, cornea, and refractive surgery is 12%. Do you know the six layers with numbers, and the dystrophy stain table?
  4. Can you write the Goldmann equation and map every glaucoma drug onto it?
  5. Can you recite Morgan norms and both AC/A formulae?
  6. Have you memorised the visual field localisation table, including congruity and macular sparing?
  7. Do you know which drugs need formal monitoring protocols: hydroxychloroquine, ethambutol, vigabatrin, pentosan polysulfate?
  8. Can you assign every ocular structure to one of four embryonic sources?
  9. Have you done at least one full 185-item timed block at 77 seconds per item?
  10. Multiple-response items are all-or-nothing and are 10 to 15% of the exam. Have you practised reading the "select 2" or "select 3" instruction before answering?
