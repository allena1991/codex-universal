How to use this guide

This guide is built around one finding that dominates the learning-science literature: you remember what you retrieve, not what you re-read. Every chapter is therefore structured so that reading is the shortest phase and self-testing is the longest. The layout does the work for you if you follow the conventions below.

Highest-return study behaviours
  1. Retrieve before you review. Close the guide and write what you know about a condition area, then read to fill gaps. This single change outperforms re-reading by a wide margin.
  2. Interleave disciplines within a condition area. Study the cornea's anatomy, physiology, pharmacology, and pathology in one sitting. That is exactly how ABS items are written: one tissue, many disciplines.
  3. Space, do not mass. Three 40-minute passes over eight days beat one 2-hour pass, even at equal total time.
  4. Explain the mechanism. For every fact, ask "so what happens if this fails?" Mechanistic elaboration is what converts a fact into an answerable item.
  5. Practise in exam conditions. 185 items in 4 hours is 77 s per item. Train at that pace or you will lose points you actually knew.

Read the blueprint correctly

Emergencies / trauma items are embedded inside the other condition areas of Normal health / disease / trauma rather than presented as a standalone block. Do not study trauma as a separate silo; study it as the acute presentation of each tissue you already cover.

Systemic health is the single largest area on the exam at roughly one item in five. Candidates routinely under-prepare it because it feels like general medicine rather than optometry. That misjudgement costs more points than any optics gap.

This guide is organised by discipline because that is how basic science is encoded in memory, and each chapter is cross-tagged to the condition areas it feeds. Use the tag chips under each chapter heading to check your blueprint coverage.

A workable revision schedule

Pick the column that matches the time you actually have, not the time you wish you had.

Three timelines, same principles
Phase10 weeks5 weeks2 weeks (salvage)
Pass 1: build Weeks 1–5. One chapter per day, objectives cold-tested first. Make no new notes beyond cloze prompts. Weeks 1–2. Two chapters per day, high-yield and trap boxes only on first read. Days 1–5. Read only the high-yield boxes, numbers panels, and recaps.
Pass 2: retrieve Weeks 6–8. All retrieval blocks, closed book. Re-read only what you miss. Weeks 3–4. All retrieval blocks + the numbers appendix daily. Days 6–10. Retrieval blocks for the six largest condition areas only.
Pass 3: simulate Weeks 9–10. Timed 185-item blocks, then error-log by condition area. Week 5. Two timed blocks, error-log, targeted repair. Days 11–13. One timed block, repair the top three error clusters.
Day before Numbers appendix, mnemonic boxes, and the recap panels. Nothing new. Sleep is a study technique.
Error log, the one extra document you should keep

For every practice item you miss, record four things: the condition area, the discipline, the specific fact you lacked, and why you chose the wrong option. The fourth column is the valuable one. Most repeated errors are reasoning habits, not knowledge gaps, and only the fourth column exposes them.
