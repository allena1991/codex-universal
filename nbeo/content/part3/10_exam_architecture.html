The three announcements, and what each means for you
AnnouncementAction
"Candidates, please enter the exam room." Enter, close the door behind you, and begin your 3 minutes of observation. The pod colour is stated in the announcement. In a skills station the standardised patient is already in the room; in an encounter station they are not, and will enter shortly before the cycle begins.
"Exam cycle [number] has begun." The scored 15 minutes starts. In encounter stations, the clinical data becomes available on your device at this moment. You may now start asking the standardised patient questions and, in skills stations, you may now touch the patient.
"Exam cycle [number] has ended, please exit the room." Stop, leave the room, and go to your designated break-area desk for the next room number. The encounter note submits automatically. Remain in the room until this plays, even if you have finished.

What you can and cannot ask
  • You may ask "where" questions at any time, during observation and during the cycle: where the lighting control is, where a supply is kept.
  • You may ask whether the examiner or proctor has a view, at any point while you are performing that view.
  • You may not ask how to use equipment or any other instructional question. It will not be answered.
  • Confirmation of a view means only that something is visible. It does not tell you whether the view is good enough, whether it meets the item criteria, or whether it is of the correct structure or quadrant.
  • Examiners comment on a view only while it is being performed. Asking afterwards gets "I can only comment on a view while it is being performed."
  • Neutral responses are not feedback. "I cannot answer that", "I do not have that information", and "it is up to you" are scripted and tell you nothing about your performance.
  • No time is added for asking and answering questions.

Observation time: use all three minutes

What you may and may not do during observation
In a skills station you mayYou may not

  • Wash your hands
  • Disinfect the slit lamp with an alcohol wipe
  • Focus the slit lamp and set the illumination
  • Try on the BIO headset and adjust pantoscopic tilt and the oculars

In an encounter station the SP is not present during observation. The overview information is limited to patient demographics, the chief complaint, the review of systems, and a current medication list, and it remains available as part of the clinical data once the cycle starts. You must at minimum use hand sanitiser before entering; hand washing is not required in encounter stations.
A three-minute observation routine, memorised
Skills station
  1. 0:00 to 0:20. Read the overview: which skills, in this station. Read the repeat policy on the door.
  2. 0:20 to 0:50. Wash your hands to CDC standard. Then do not touch your face or hair.
  3. 0:50 to 1:20. Disinfect the slit lamp headrest and chin rest with an alcohol wipe. Locate the anaesthetic, fluorescein, cushioning solution, cotton-tipped applicators, tissues, and waste bin.
  4. 1:20 to 2:20. Focus the slit lamp on the headrest bar, set your oculars to your own refraction, set the interpupillary distance, and set the beam: low magnification, wide diffuse beam to start, and check that you can produce a parallelepiped and an optic section quickly.
  5. 2:20 to 2:50. If this is the posterior station, fit the BIO headset, set the pantoscopic tilt and the oculars, and confirm you can see clearly at 18 to 25 inches. Locate the condensing lens and the non-contact fundus lens. Practise the room lighting control.
  6. 2:50 to 3:00. Rehearse your opening sentence silently, and decide which skill you will start with.
Encounter station
  1. 0:00 to 0:30. Sanitise your hands. Read the demographics and the chief complaint. Say the likely differential to yourself.
  2. 0:30 to 1:30. Read the review of systems and the medication list carefully. Every medication is a clue: it is the cause, a contraindication, or a diagnostic pointer.
  3. 1:30 to 2:30. On the note board, write your history plan: the three or four HPI questions that will most efficiently separate your differential, plus the standing prompts (ocular, medical, medication, allergy, surgical, family, social).
  4. 2:30 to 3:00. Rehearse your opening: introduce yourself by OE TRACKER number, confirm the patient, and state what you are going to do.

Interacting with the standardised patient
  • Address them as Mr. Lee or Ms. Lee. Introduce yourself by your OE TRACKER number.
  • Handshakes are not permitted, for hygiene.
  • Do not touch the patient below the shoulder. Be mindful of personal space, especially at the slit lamp.
  • Respond to the patient being portrayed, not to the SP's real appearance. A forty-year-old woman may be portraying an elderly man who wears glasses. If the case is a child, the SP will tell you they are the parent or guardian.
  • Casual conversation may occur only if you initiate it, and there is a list of topics that must not be discussed: NBEO or NCCTO, the exam, the SP's job, your performance, and your school.
  • If you need to read the device for a while, you may say "Please give me a moment to review some information on my device." This is not required, and you are not penalised for using the device, but it eases the silence and the SPs are trained to expect pauses.
  • To dismiss the SP in an encounter station, say something explicit such as "Thank you, Ms. Lee, you may check out." "Thank you" alone will not do it. Once they leave they cannot return, so only dismiss them when you are certain you have finished all questions and all patient education.
  • In a skills station, if you want to end scoring early, say "I am finished with this station." The examiner then leaves and no further items are scored. This is your choice, and there is rarely a reason to do it.
  • Stay in the room until the ending announcement plays regardless.

Incident reports and irregularities

  • File on your device during the break immediately after the affected station. Reports are not accepted once you have left the exam hallway.
  • Document anything you believe negatively affected your performance: equipment, technology, the examiner, proctor, or SP, or a problem with your own performance caused by an irregularity.
  • Spelling and grammar do not matter. Content does. Be specific about what happened, when, and how it affected you.
  • All reports are reviewed. Staff may review the recording or interview the SP or examiner, and may speak with you after the session. If they do so in an exam room, assume it is recorded.
  • A redo may be offered for a skills station only, and only for an administrative irregularity such as equipment failure, power loss, or an SP problem, limited to the affected skill or station, and only on the day of your exam. A redo is never offered for a patient encounter station.
  • During the cycle, if equipment fails, tell the SP, who will summon staff. If extra time is granted it appears in the top left corner of your device.

  3. Nothing. You may not perform any action on the SP, including asking them to move into the slit lamp.

Chapter recap
  1. Twelve stations, ten encounters and two skills stations, 15 minutes each, with a 24 minute break after cycle 8.
  2. Three minutes of observation is free preparation time: wash, disinfect, focus, and plan.
  3. You may not touch the patient during observation, and you may not open packages.
  4. Only "where" questions and view confirmations are answered; a confirmed view means only that something is visible.
  5. Introduce yourself by OE TRACKER number, address the patient as Mr. or Ms. Lee, no handshake, nothing below the shoulder.
  6. Dismiss the SP explicitly, and only when you are certain you are finished.
  7. Use the platform timer; there are no watches and no reminders.
  8. The slit lamp camera is on the left ocular, so what you see through the left ocular is what is scored.
  9. File incident reports in the break immediately after the affected station.
  10. No communication with other candidates once you are in the hallway, and everything is recorded.
