How to use this guide

Part III PEPS is unlike the two examinations before it. Nothing is multiple choice. You are scored on what you say out loud and what you physically do, item by item, against a published checklist, while a licensed optometrist watches and a camera records what you see through the ocular.

That has one dominant consequence for how you prepare: knowing the answer is worth nothing unless you verbalise it, in the right words, within the fifteen minutes. Candidates who fail rarely fail on knowledge. They fail because they examined a structure without naming it, described a finding as "normal" instead of describing it, lost a view and did not troubleshoot, ran out of time, or wrote a plan without a timeframe.

Three structural facts that should shape your entire preparation
  1. Fifty-four percent of your score comes from the encounter note. Clinical Assessment and Interpretation plus Management and Documentation total 54 of 100, and both are captured through the electronic note. If you can examine beautifully but write a vague plan, you will struggle. Practise writing plans, under time pressure, in a text box.
  2. Skills is only twenty-two percent, and the case history is part of it. The two skills stations matter, but they are not the majority of the exam, and a substantial part of the Skills domain is earned by taking a thorough focused history in the other ten stations. Do not spend all your preparation at a slit lamp.
  3. Patient Education plus Communication is twenty-four percent, and it is earned entirely by talking. That is more than the physical skills. Rehearse explaining ten diagnoses out loud, in plain language, to a non-clinician.

How to rehearse, and why rehearsal beats reading here

  1. Say everything out loud, always. From the first week of preparation, never practise a skill silently. The examiner cannot award an item for a finding you noticed but did not state, and the habit of narrating has to be automatic before you are under pressure.
  2. Rehearse against the actual checklist. The evaluation forms are published. The appendix reproduces them as checklists. Have a classmate score you item by item, aloud, and count the misses.
  3. Always practise on a clock. Every station is exactly 15 minutes. Set a timer for every drill from the beginning. Time is the single commonest reason candidates lose items they knew.
  4. Practise on unfamiliar people. The standardised patients are strangers, they are trained to be neutral, they may blink or ask for a break, and they may be portraying a person quite unlike themselves. Practise on people you have not examined before.
  5. Rehearse the note in a text box, not on paper. You will type the assessment and plan on a laptop while a person sits in front of you. Practise typing a complete plan in under 4 minutes.
  6. Rehearse the patient explanation on a non-clinician. If your partner or flatmate cannot restate what you told them, the standardised patient will not be able to either.
  7. Build a fixed sequence for each skill and never vary it. Under stress you will fall back on habit. Make the habit the checklist order.
The six failure modes, ranked
  1. Silence. Examining a structure without naming it and describing it.
  2. Vague description. Saying "normal", "fine", "OK", "unremarkable", or "within normal limits" instead of describing what you see.
  3. Running out of time, usually because of an over-long history or a lost view that was not troubleshot quickly.
  4. An incomplete plan. No dose, no duration, no referral timeframe, or no return-to-clinic interval.
  5. Losing or never obtaining the view, and not asking whether the examiner has a view.
  6. Hygiene and safety breaks, most often touching the face or hair after washing hands, or failing to state that instruments would be disinfected.

Every one of these is a rehearsal problem rather than a knowledge problem, and every one is fixable in the weeks before the exam.

A note on currency. NBEO updates the candidate guide, the blueprint, and the evaluation forms annually. This guide reflects the August 2026 to May 2027 documents. Download the current versions from the NBEO website when you register and reconcile them against the checklists in the appendix, because item numbering and wording do change between administrations.
