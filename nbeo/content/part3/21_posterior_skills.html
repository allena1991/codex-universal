Station rules you must know before you start
  • All items are performed on ONE eye. Ask which eye at the start of each skill.
  • The SP sets the BIO rheostat at their calibrated setting. Do not adjust it.
  • Optimal working distance is 18 to 25 inches, and the camera records the image you view through either ocular.
  • A "peripheral view" is defined: an area of retina extending from the equator to the ora serrata and containing at least a partial view of a vortex vein ampulla. That definition is the target for every one of the eight BIO quadrant items.
  • Fill the condensing lens as much as possible without the arcades in view. If you can see the arcades, you are too posterior for a peripheral item.
  • BIO may be performed seated or reclined, with the patient's consent.
  • Be efficient with light. Do not shine light on the same area for an extended period. The SP may blink or ask for a break, and you should accommodate that.
  • For retroillumination of the crystalline lens only (item 25), the light source must be on maximum illumination and you must move the light source to obtain the retroilluminated image.
  • Maintain hygiene throughout (item 44).

Skill 4 · Binocular indirect ophthalmoscopy (items 1 to 21)

Opening (item 1) Good morning, Ms. Lee. I am candidate [OE TRACKER number]. Which eye am I examining today? I am going to use this headset and a hand-held lens to look at the inside of the back of your eye, particularly the outer edges of the retina, which is the light-sensitive lining. The light is bright and it will be uncomfortable, but it does not touch your eye and it will not harm it. You can blink whenever you need to, and tell me if you need a break at any point. Would you prefer to sit up or lie back for this?

That paragraph earns item 1, obtains consent for positioning, and pre-empts the discomfort that otherwise causes the patient to squeeze and lose you views.

20Perform the skill in a safe manner for the patient Do not brace on the patient's face or lean on them. Do not sustain the beam on one area. Warn before each move. Accept blinks and breaks. Do not touch below the shoulder. Keep the light off the fovea for prolonged periods.
21Perform in a smooth and logical sequence Go around the periphery in one continuous rotation, either clockwise or anticlockwise, then finish centrally. Do not skip and return.
BIO technique points that determine whether you get a view at all
  1. Set the headset during observation time. Adjust the pantoscopic tilt so the mirrors align with your pupils, set the ocular separation, and confirm you can see clearly at 18 to 25 inches. Do this before the cycle starts.
  2. Stand opposite the quadrant you want. To see the superior retina you stand below and the patient looks up. The lens must be positioned so the light enters and returns through the pupil along the axis of that quadrant.
  3. Hold the condensing lens with the more convex surface toward the patient, about 2 inches (roughly the focal length) from the eye, and align lens, pupil, and your line of sight.
  4. Find the image with the lens close, then withdraw slowly until the image fills the lens. Small movements toward and away from the eye change magnification and field; small lateral tilts kill the image.
  5. Move the lens, not your head, for fine adjustment.
  6. If you lose the view, do not freeze. Reduce the working distance, re-find the pupil, then withdraw. Ask "Do you have a view?" while performing it, and troubleshoot for anything obstructing the central camera.
  7. Remember the image is inverted and reversed. When the patient looks up and you view from below, you are seeing the superior retina, and it appears inverted in the lens. Name the anatomic quadrant, not what appears at the top of your view.
  8. Look for the vortex vein ampulla deliberately. It is the defined marker that your view is genuinely peripheral, and naming it demonstrates the view meets the criterion.

Skill 5 · Dilated biomicroscopy and non-contact fundus lens (items 22 to 43)

This skill has two halves. Items 23 to 28 are performed with the biomicroscope alone, no fundus lens: the crystalline lens (direct and retroillumination) and the retrolental space and anterior vitreous. Items 29 to 42 are performed with the non-contact fundus lens: posterior vitreous, optic nerve, arcades, and macula.

Part A: biomicroscope alone (items 22 to 28)
ItemWhat is scoredTechnique and words
22Explain the purpose to the patient "Now I am going to use the microscope to look at the lens inside your eye and the jelly behind it, and then I will hold a lens in front of your eye to look at the optic nerve and the central retina. Nothing will touch your eye. The light is bright, and you can blink whenever you need to."
23Properly scan all layers of the crystalline lens Parallelepiped or optic section, 16×, angle 30 to 45 degrees. Focus progressively through every layer and say each one: "I am scanning through all layers of the crystalline lens: anterior capsule, anterior cortex, the adult, infantile, fetal and embryonic nuclei, posterior cortex, and posterior capsule." Move the focus in a controlled anteroposterior sweep, not a single plane.
24Accurately describe the crystalline lens "The crystalline lens is clear throughout all layers, with a clear anterior and posterior capsule, no nuclear sclerosis, no cortical spoking, no posterior subcapsular changes, no vacuoles, and no pseudoexfoliative material on the anterior capsule." If cataract is present, grade it by type and severity, for example "grade 2 nuclear sclerosis with trace cortical spoking inferonasally."
25Properly examine the lens using retroillumination · maximum illumination for this item only, and you must move the light source Turn the illumination to maximum, use a moderate beam, and displace the illumination arm so the beam passes through the pupil to the retina and returns, backlighting the lens against the fundus reflex. Move the light source across the pupil to retro-illuminate the whole lens. Announce all of it: "I am now using retroillumination, with the illumination at maximum, and moving the light source across the pupil to backlight the lens against the fundus reflex."
26Accurately describe the lens on retroillumination "On retroillumination the lens is clear with an even, unbroken fundus reflex. There are no cortical spokes, no posterior subcapsular opacities, no vacuoles, and no lens opacities silhouetted against the reflex."
27Properly examine the anterior vitreous Return to normal illumination, focus posterior to the lens into the retrolental space and anterior vitreous, with a conical or narrow beam and higher illumination, in a darkened room. "I am now focusing behind the lens into the retrolental space and anterior vitreous."
28Accurately describe the anterior vitreous "The retrolental space and anterior vitreous are optically clear with no cells, no pigment granules, no haemorrhage, no asteroid hyalosis, and no anterior vitreous strands."
Part B: with the non-contact fundus lens (items 29 to 43)
ItemWhat is scoredTechnique and words
29Provide appropriate instructions for fixation "Please keep your chin on the rest and your forehead forward, keep both eyes open, and look straight ahead over my shoulder at the corner of the wall. Keep looking at that spot even though the light is bright, and blink whenever you need to. If I need you to look in a different direction I will tell you."

33Properly examine the optic nerve Centre the disc, use a beam narrower than the disc for contour assessment, and scan the whole disc and the peripapillary area. Announce: "I am now examining the optic nerve head."
34Accurately describe the optic nerve: colour, rim integrity, contour, PPA All four named features must be stated. "The optic nerve head is pink and healthy in colour with no pallor. The neuroretinal rim is intact throughout with no notching or focal thinning, and rim thickness follows the ISNT rule with inferior thicker than superior, thicker than nasal, thicker than temporal. The contour of the cup is regular with a gradual slope and no undermining or laminar dot sign. There is no peripapillary atrophy and no disc haemorrhage."
35Accurately state the horizontal cup-to-disc ratio State it as a separate, explicit number. "The horizontal cup-to-disc ratio is 0.3."
36Accurately state the vertical cup-to-disc ratio "The vertical cup-to-disc ratio is 0.3." Two separate items: state two separate numbers.
37Properly examine and obtain a clear image of the inferior and superior temporal arcades Follow each arcade from the disc out toward the periphery. Ask the patient to look slightly temporally if needed. "I am now following the superior temporal arcade out from the disc, and now the inferior temporal arcade."
38Accurately identify and assess the temporal arcades "The superior and inferior temporal arcades are of normal calibre and course, with a normal arteriole-to-venule ratio of approximately two to three, no arteriovenous nicking, no copper or silver wiring, no tortuosity, no sheathing, no emboli, no haemorrhage, no exudate, and no cotton wool spots along either arcade."
39Properly examine and obtain a clear image of the inferior and superior nasal arcades Ask the patient to look slightly nasally. "Please look a little toward your nose. I am now following the superior nasal arcade, and now the inferior nasal arcade."
40Accurately identify and assess the nasal arcades "The superior and inferior nasal arcades are of normal calibre and course with a normal arteriole-to-venule ratio, no nicking, no haemorrhage, no exudate, and no cotton wool spots."
41Properly examine both the fovea and the macula Two structures, both required. Narrow the beam and reduce illumination for patient comfort, and warn: "This next part is the brightest, and it will only take a few seconds. Please keep looking straight at my shoulder. I am now examining the macula, and now the fovea."
42Accurately describe both the fovea and the macula Describe them separately. "The macula is flat and intact with even pigmentation, no drusen, no pigmentary mottling, no haemorrhage, no exudate, and no oedema or cystic change. The fovea is flat with a crisp foveal reflex present and no atrophy, no pigment change, and no lamellar or full-thickness defect."
43Smooth and logical sequence Lens layers, lens retroillumination, anterior vitreous, then the fundus lens: posterior vitreous, optic nerve with both cup ratios, temporal arcades, nasal arcades, then macula and fovea last (because it is the brightest and least comfortable).
44Maintain proper hygiene throughout the station Wash hands during observation, then do not touch your face or hair. Disinfect the headrest and chin rest and the fundus lens. If you contaminate your hands, wash them again before touching the patient or the contact surfaces.
The ten items most commonly lost in this station
  1. Peripheral views with the arcades in view. If you can see the arcades, that is not a peripheral view and the item is not earned. Push out further until a vortex ampulla appears.
  2. Not naming the vortex vein ampulla, which is the defined marker of a genuine peripheral view.
  3. Naming the wrong quadrant, because the image is inverted and reversed. Name the anatomic quadrant based on where the patient is looking and where you are standing.
  4. Adjusting the BIO rheostat, which the guide explicitly tells you not to do.
  5. Item 25: performing retroillumination without turning the illumination to maximum and without moving the light source.
  6. Item 23: focusing on the lens in one plane instead of scanning through all layers and naming them.
  7. Items 35 and 36: giving one combined cup-to-disc ratio instead of two separate numbers.
  8. Item 34: describing the disc without covering all four required features (colour, rim integrity, contour, and peripapillary atrophy).
  9. Items 39 and 40: forgetting the nasal arcades entirely after doing the temporal ones.
  10. Items 41 and 42: describing the macula but not the fovea separately.
A fifteen-minute pacing plan for the posterior station
ClockTask
0:00–0:40Introduce, ask which eye, explain BIO, obtain positioning consent, confirm the patient is comfortable.
0:40–7:00BIO. Eight quadrants in one continuous rotation, roughly 40 seconds each, naming the quadrant, the vortex ampulla, and the assessment. Then the posterior pole.
7:00–9:30Biomicroscope alone. Explain, scan all lens layers, retroillumination at maximum with the light source moving, anterior vitreous.
9:30–14:30Fundus lens. Fixation instructions, lens positioning, posterior vitreous, optic nerve with both cup ratios, temporal arcades, nasal arcades, macula and fovea last.
14:30–15:00Buffer. Remain in the room until the announcement.

Forty seconds per quadrant is the discipline that makes this station work. Practise with a timer and a partner calling out the elapsed time until eight quadrants in six and a half minutes feels unhurried.

Key
  1. An area of retina extending from the equator to the ora serrata and containing at least a partial view of a vortex vein ampulla.
  2. The arcades must not be in view; the condensing lens should be filled as much as possible, and a vortex vein ampulla should be visible.
  3. Stand at the patient's superior side, above the head; the patient looks down.
  4. 18 to 25 inches; do not adjust the BIO rheostat, which the SP has set to its calibrated level.
  5. Eight peripheral quadrants, each generating two items: one for obtaining the view and one for identifying the quadrant and giving an assessment, plus two more items for the posterior pole.
  6. Item 25, lens retroillumination; it also requires that you move the light source to obtain the retroilluminated image.
  7. Anterior capsule, anterior cortex, adult nucleus, infantile nucleus, fetal nucleus, embryonic nucleus, posterior cortex, and posterior capsule.
  8. Items 23 to 28: crystalline lens by direct illumination, crystalline lens by retroillumination, and the retrolental space and anterior vitreous.
  9. Colour, rim integrity, contour, and peripapillary atrophy.
  10. Two, as separate explicit numbers: the horizontal ratio and the vertical ratio, each its own item.
  11. The nasal arcades; ask the patient to look slightly toward the nose.
  12. Because it is the brightest and least comfortable part, so doing it last preserves patient cooperation for everything else.
  13. Move the lens closer to re-find the pupil then withdraw slowly; keep the lens perpendicular and centred and move the lens rather than your head; ask "Do you have a view?" while performing it and check nothing is obstructing the central camera.
  14. Because the BIO image is inverted and reversed. Name the anatomic quadrant based on where the patient is looking and where you are standing, not on where the feature appears within the lens.
  15. About 40 seconds, giving eight quadrants plus the posterior pole in roughly six and a half minutes.
Chapter recap
  1. A peripheral view runs from the equator to the ora and contains a vortex vein ampulla; name the ampulla.
  2. No arcades in the eight peripheral items; fill the lens as much as possible.
  3. Stand opposite the quadrant, patient looks toward it, and name the anatomic quadrant despite the inverted image.
  4. Work distance 18 to 25 inches, and never touch the rheostat.
  5. Forty seconds per quadrant, one continuous rotation, then the posterior pole.
  6. Items 23 to 28 use the biomicroscope alone; retroillumination needs maximum illumination and a moving light source.
  7. Scan and name all lens layers, not one plane.
  8. The optic nerve needs colour, rim, contour, and PPA, then two separate cup-to-disc numbers.
  9. Do the nasal arcades as well as the temporal, and describe the fovea separately from the macula.
  10. Macula and fovea last, and hygiene throughout.
