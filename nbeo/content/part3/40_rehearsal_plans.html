Chapter 9Rehearsal plans and timed drills

Everything here is done out loud and on a clock
Five rules for every practice session
  1. Out loud, always. Never rehearse a skill silently. The verbalisation is the score.
  2. On a timer, always. Fifteen minutes, from the first practice session onward.
  3. Against the checklist. Use the appendix forms and have a partner tick items as you perform them, then count the misses.
  4. On unfamiliar people. Rotate partners. Practise on people whose fundus you have not memorised.
  5. Log every miss. Keep one page per skill listing the item numbers you lose, and target those specifically in the next session.

Eight-week plan

The full preparation arc
WeekFocusSessions
1Learn the forms Read the current candidate guide, blueprint, and both evaluation forms end to end. Transcribe the evaluation forms into your own checklist. Watch the NBEO orientation video and complete the software tutorial. Write out your own scripts for the five skills, in your own words, and read them aloud until they are fluent.
2Biomicroscopy, untimed then timed Three sessions. First untimed, narrating every one of items 2 to 25 while a partner ticks. Then two timed runs at 6 minutes. Drill upper lid eversion twenty times until it takes eight seconds. Drill Van Herick and iris transillumination separately, ten repetitions each.
3Gonioscopy and applanation Three sessions. Gonioscopy: mirror-to-quadrant naming drill on paper, then twenty lens insertions and removals, then four full timed runs at 4.5 minutes covering all four quadrants with structure naming. Applanation: ten full timed runs at 4 minutes, including stating the pressure with the time and educating the patient.
4Full anterior station Four complete 15-minute anterior stations with a partner scoring all 59 items. Target: fewer than five misses by the end of the week. Include a deliberate "lost view" drill where your partner tells you they have no view, so you practise troubleshooting under time.
5BIO Four sessions. First, quadrant geography drill: for each of the eight quadrants, state where you stand and where the patient looks, on paper, until instant. Then eight-quadrant runs at 40 seconds per quadrant with a partner calling the time. Specifically drill finding and naming a vortex vein ampulla in every quadrant.
6Dilated biomicroscopy and fundus lens Four sessions. Drill lens layer scanning with verbalisation, retroillumination at maximum illumination with a moving light source, and fundus lens positioning. Then full runs of items 22 to 43 at 7 minutes, insisting on both cup-to-disc numbers, the nasal arcades, and a separate fovea description.
7Full posterior station, and encounter drills begin Three complete 15-minute posterior stations scoring all 44 items. In parallel, begin encounter station drills: three per session, using the case categories in Chapter 4, with a partner playing the SP from a written script.
8Full mock session and polish One full twelve-station mock if you can arrange it, or at minimum a six-station half-session, with real timing and real breaks. Then targeted repair of your top five recurring misses. Two days before, stop practising skills and review only your scripts, the lexicon, and the plan templates.

Four-week plan

WeekContent
1Read the guide and both forms. Build your checklists. Write and memorise your five scripts. Three biomicroscopy runs and three applanation runs, timed.
2Gonioscopy geography and technique to fluency. Two full timed anterior stations scored against the checklist. Begin BIO quadrant geography drills.
3BIO eight-quadrant runs at 40 seconds each. Fundus lens runs. Two full timed posterior stations scored. Six encounter drills, focusing on the four-minute history and the four-minute note.
4One anterior and one posterior full station on each of three days, plus six encounter drills. Final two days: scripts, lexicon, and plan templates only.

One-week plan, for when time has run out

DayContent
1Read both evaluation forms aloud, item by item, and write your script for each of the five skills. Read the candidate guide's rules on views, repeats, and stops.
2One timed biomicroscopy run and one timed applanation run, scored. Drill upper lid eversion, Van Herick, and iris transillumination twenty times each.
3Gonioscopy: mirror-to-quadrant drill, then three timed runs naming quadrant, most posterior structure, and iris approach.
4One full timed anterior station, scored, then repair the misses immediately in a second run.
5BIO quadrant geography drill, then two eight-quadrant timed runs. Fundus lens run covering items 29 to 43.
6One full timed posterior station, scored. Then four encounter drills: history in four minutes and a complete typed plan in four minutes.
7Rest. Read your scripts, the lexicon, and the plan templates once. Sleep.

Individual drills

Ten drills, with the target
DrillTargetMethod
Upper lid eversion8 seconds, 20 for 20 With a cotton-tipped applicator, on five different partners. Instruct, evert, sweep medial to lateral, describe, release, and have them blink. Count failures.
Whole-cornea sweep30 seconds each Parallelepiped limbus to limbus in overlapping passes, then optic section limbus to limbus, narrating both.
Van Herick15 seconds Beam at 60 degrees at the temporal limbus, state the shadow-to-thickness ratio and the grade. Ten repetitions on five people, comparing your grades against a partner's.
Cells and flare20 seconds Darken the room, small bright conical beam, focus in the aqueous, state grade zero cells and grade zero flare using SUN language.
Iris transillumination20 seconds Beam through the pupil, moved across it, covering the whole iris; narrate the technique and the finding as separate items.
Gonioscopy quadrant namingInstant Paper drill: partner says "temporal mirror", you say "nasal angle". Then reverse. Fifty repetitions. Then on the lens.
Applanation mire endpoint45 seconds per eye From prism contact to a stated reading with the time. Have your partner deliberately over- or under-fluoresce so you learn to recognise and correct mire width.
BIO quadrant geographyInstant Partner names a quadrant, you state where you stand and where the patient looks, and then do it. Eight quadrants, ten cycles.
Vortex ampulla hunt8 for 8 In each quadrant, push out until an ampulla is visible, hold it, and name it. This single drill is the difference between eight scored peripheral views and eight unscored ones.
Four-minute typed plan4 minutes Partner reads a case from Chapter 4. You type a complete plan on a laptop: medical, non-medical, referral with specialty and timeframe, additional testing with indications, patient education with mechanism and warning signs, and a single RTC interval. Score it against the template.
The four-minute history drill, which most candidates skip

Have a partner sit opposite you with a written script for one of the cases in Chapter 4. They answer only what is on the script, and say "I do not have that information" for anything else, exactly as the SP will. Set a timer for 4 minutes.

Score yourself on coverage: did you obtain the HPI in full, past ocular history including correction and compliance, medical history with medications and allergies, surgical history, family ocular and medical history, and social history including occupation, driving, smoking, and eye protection? Six categories in four minutes.

Do this drill fifteen times across your preparation. It builds the sequence into muscle memory, and the sequence is what protects you when the case is unfamiliar and you feel the time pressure.

The week before, and the day itself

Logistics and mindset
WhenDo
One week before Download and re-read the current candidate guide, blueprint, and both evaluation forms and reconcile them with your checklists, because item numbering changes between administrations. Confirm your registration date and time. Book travel and accommodation close to the NCCTO. Complete the software tutorial again.
Two days before Stop practising skills. Read your scripts, the lexicon, and the plan templates. Pack: lab coat, professional clothes and closed shoes, two forms of identification (one government photo ID), your OE TRACKER number and NBEO password, an eye patch if you use one, permitted small items, and a labelled drink and snack for the hallway bins.
Night before Sleep. Do not review anything after the evening. Set two alarms. Plan to arrive at the building no earlier than 15 minutes before your listed time.
Morning Eat properly. Tie hair back. Wear your glasses if you wore them in your check-in photograph. Use the restroom before the session starts, because no extra time is given.
During orientation Handle the actual laptop, BIO headset, and slit lamp. Adjust the BIO headset to your own pantoscopic tilt and ocular separation and remember the settings. Note where supplies are kept. Ask staff any equipment question now, because instructional questions will not be answered later.
Between stations Sit at your designated desk for the next room number. File an incident report if needed. Drink water, eat if you need to, use the restroom. Do not replay the last station. Take three slow breaths and reset.
After cycle 8 Use the 24-minute break properly: eat something, move, and rehydrate. Fatigue in the last four stations is a real and preventable cause of lost items.
At the end File any final incident report at your desk and remain seated until dismissed. Reports are not accepted once you leave the hallway.
The ten sentences to have memorised verbatim
  1. "I am candidate [OE TRACKER number]. Which eye am I examining today?"
  2. "Before I begin, I would properly disinfect all ocular instruments prior to using them."
  3. "Please look all the way down and keep looking down. I am going to turn your upper eyelid out for a moment."
  4. "I am performing a Van Herick assessment at the temporal limbus; the shadow depth is greater than one half of the corneal section thickness, grade 4."
  5. "I am transilluminating the entire iris by directing the beam through the pupil."
  6. "I am viewing the inferior angle through the superior mirror. The most posterior visible structure is the ciliary body band, the iris approach is regular and flat, Shaffer grade 4."
  7. "The intraocular pressure of the right eye by Goldmann applanation is 16 millimetres of mercury at [time]."
  8. "Your eye pressure today was 16, which is in the normal range. Pressure matters because pressure that is too high over time can damage the nerve at the back of the eye."
  9. "I am viewing the superior-temporal peripheral retina, from the equator to the ora serrata, with a vortex vein ampulla in view. It is flat and intact with no holes, tears, or lattice degeneration."
  10. "Horizontal cup-to-disc ratio 0.3. Vertical cup-to-disc ratio 0.3."

Plus two for the encounter stations: "Please give me a moment to review some information on my device" and "Thank you, Ms. Lee, you may check out."

Chapter recap
  1. Out loud, on a clock, against the checklist, on unfamiliar people, with a log of misses.
  2. Eight weeks if you have them, four if you do not, one if that is all there is.
  3. Upper lid eversion, Van Herick, iris transillumination, and the vortex ampulla hunt are the four highest-yield individual drills.
  4. Forty seconds per BIO quadrant is the pacing that makes the posterior station work.
  5. Practise typing a complete plan in four minutes, fifteen times.
  6. Practise a six-category history in four minutes, fifteen times.
  7. Download the current forms in the week before, because item numbering changes.
  8. Use orientation time to set up the actual BIO headset and slit lamp.
  9. Use the 24-minute break to eat and reset, because fatigue costs items in the last four stations.
  10. Memorise the ten sentences verbatim, so they arrive without effort under pressure.
