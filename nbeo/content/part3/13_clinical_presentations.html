Chapter 4The nine clinical presentation categories

Anterior 17 · Posterior 16 · Glaucoma 14 Systemic 11 · Refraction 11 · Neuro 9 Contact lens 8 · Binocular 8 · Pediatrics 6

Every one of your ten patient encounters is drawn from these nine categories, and each version of the exam samples them according to the blueprint weights. For each category below: the cases you are most likely to meet, the history questions that separate them, the ancillary tests to order, and the plan elements that must appear. Conditions were selected for frequency and criticality, exactly as the blueprint describes, so expect either a typical common presentation or a pathognomonic dangerous one.

3. Glaucoma · weight 14

CaseHistory that clinches itPlan essentials
Primary open-angle glaucoma, newFamily history, race, steroid use, sleep apnoea, migraine, prior trauma, nocturnal hypotension Pachymetry, gonioscopy, 24-2 fields, OCT RNFL and ganglion cell, disc photography; latanoprost nightly or discuss SLT as first-line (LiGHT); target IOP stated; family screening advice; RTC 6 weeks.
Acute angle closureSevere pain, nausea and vomiting, haloes, hyperopia, anticholinergic use, dim light onset Full protocol in order, pilocarpine only once IOP is under 40 to 50 mmHg; bilateral laser iridotomy; consider lens extraction (EAGLE); RTC 1 day.
Normal-tension glaucomaCold hands, migraine, low blood pressure or nocturnal dipping, anaemia, sleep apnoea 30% IOP reduction target (CNTGS); MRI if the picture is atypical; 24-hour blood pressure and sleep apnoea assessment; RTC 6 weeks.
Pseudoexfoliation glaucomaAsymmetry, poor dilation, prior cataract surgery, systemic vascular history Gonioscopy documenting the Sampaolesi line; SLT works well; lower target and closer follow-up; warn about surgical risks and late IOL subluxation; RTC 6 weeks.
Steroid-induced ocular hypertensionSteroid by any route including inhaled, nasal, dermatologic, or intravitreal implant Reduce or substitute the steroid with the prescriber; loteprednol if a topical steroid is still needed; aqueous suppressants; RTC 2 weeks.
Angle recession glaucomaRemote blunt trauma, often decades earlier, unilateral Gonioscopy documenting recession; medical therapy (trabeculoplasty responds poorly); annual lifelong surveillance; examine the fellow eye; RTC 6 weeks then annually.
Neovascular glaucomaDiabetes, prior vein occlusion, carotid disease, pain, rapid onset Anti-VEGF plus panretinal photocoagulation referral urgently; aqueous suppressants and steroid; avoid miotics and prostaglandins; RTC 1 day.
Progressing glaucoma at "acceptable" pressureAdherence, instillation technique, cost, missed doses, diurnal variation Assess adherence and technique before escalating; lower the target; add or switch therapy or offer SLT; consider diurnal curve; RTC 6 weeks.

5. Refraction · weight 11

CaseHistory that clinches itPlan essentials
Uncorrected hyperopia with asthenopiaHeadache with near work, blur that clears on looking away, worse late in the day Cycloplegic refraction; prescribe, written out or referenced to the data, with wear instructions; explain the mechanism; RTC 6 weeks.
PresbyopiaWorking distance, occupation, monitor distance, hobbies, prior add, current spectacles Determine the add by at least two methods; verify the range of clear vision brackets the actual task; choose the design for the task; explain adaptation; RTC 2 to 4 weeks if a new progressive design.
Anisometropia with aniseikoniaPulling sensation, difficulty judging steps, prior surgery, when it started Reduce the disparity: adjust base curve, thickness and vertex distance, or fit contact lenses; consider slab-off for vertical imbalance at near; RTC 4 weeks.
Progressing myopia in a childRate of change, parental myopia, outdoor time, near work habits, age of onset Full correction, never undercorrected; axial length if available; offer orthokeratology, dual-focus soft lenses, defocus spectacles, or low-dose atropine; at least 2 hours outdoors daily; explain the long-term risk rationale; RTC 6 months.
Refractive instability in diabetesHgbA1c, recent change in medication or control, fluctuation through the day Do not prescribe; explain why; refer for control optimisation; RTC 4 to 6 weeks once control is stable.
Spectacle non-adaptationExactly what is wrong and when; what changed from the previous pair Verify against prescription and ANSI tolerances; measure monocular PDs and heights; check base curve, material, tilt, and vertex; re-refract; check binocularity and the ocular surface; RTC 2 weeks.
Post-refractive-surgery patientType and date of surgery, pre-surgical refraction, night vision, dryness, fluctuation Topography; dry eye management; document and supply pre-surgical data for future IOL calculation; explain the IOP measurement caveat; RTC 6 months.

9. Pediatrics · weight 6

Remember: the SP may be the parent

If the case is a child, the standardised patient will tell you they are the parent or guardian. Direct the history and, critically, the patient education to them as the parent of the child described. Explain in terms a parent needs: what it is, what it means for the child's vision and development, exactly what they have to do at home, how compliance will be checked, and what happens if it is not treated.

Chapter recap
  1. Nine categories, weighted; expect either a typical common case or a pathognomonic dangerous one.
  2. Anterior segment is the heaviest category, and most of its cases are treated by you rather than referred.
  3. Posterior segment cases usually hinge on one urgent decision: refer today, refer within days, or observe with education.
  4. Glaucoma cases test target setting, adherence assessment, and the mechanism of closure.
  5. Systemic cases test whether you measure blood pressure, write to the physician, and state the urgency.
  6. Refraction cases test prescribing judgement, including when not to prescribe.
  7. Neuro cases contain the same-day emergencies: giant cell arteritis, pupil-involving third nerve palsy, painful Horner, and papilloedema.
  8. Contact lens cases test differentiation of infiltrative events and hygiene counselling.
  9. Binocular cases test the CITT answer and the exclusion of neurologic mimics.
  10. Pediatric cases may be delivered through a parent, and leukocoria and congenital glaucoma are the two emergencies.
