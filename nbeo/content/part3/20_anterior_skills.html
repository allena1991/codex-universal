Chapter 5Anterior segment skills station, item by item

Biomicroscopy · items 2–25 4-mirror gonioscopy · items 26–41 Goldmann applanation · items 42–58 General items 1 and 59
Station rules you must know before you start
  • All three skills are performed on ONE eye. Ask the SP which eye at the start of each skill, and ask again if you forget.
  • You may alter the order of the skills within the station, and you may alter the order of items within a skill provided your sequence makes logical sense.
  • Item 1 is a stated intention, not an action: you must say that you would properly disinfect the ocular instruments before using them. The gonioscopy lens and the tonometer probe are to be assumed already cleaned, but you must still state that you would disinfect them.
  • State every structure as you view it, and describe every finding as you would enter it into a medical record. Half of the items on this form are "accurately describe" items, and they are earned only by speaking.
  • You are scored on what the camera captures through the LEFT ocular. Ensure the image is completely seen through the left ocular.
  • Views must not be fleeting, and "entire" means 360 degrees or the complete structure.
  • Assume the patient has less than three dioptres of corneal cylinder for tonometry, so no prism rotation is required.
  • Maintain hygiene throughout (item 59). Touching your face or hair after washing breaks protocol.

Opening the station

First 20 seconds of the cycle Good morning, Ms. Lee. I am candidate [OE TRACKER number]. Today I will be examining the front of your eye with this microscope, then looking at the drainage angle inside your eye with a small lens, and then measuring the pressure in your eye. Which eye am I examining today? Before I begin, I would properly disinfect all ocular instruments prior to using them, including the biomicroscope headrest and chin rest, the gonioscopy lens, and the tonometer probe.

That second sentence earns item 1. Say it explicitly, at the start, and say it once. Do not assume that wiping the headrest during observation substitutes for saying it.

Skill 1 · Biomicroscopy (items 2 to 25)

Item-by-item script and technique
ItemWhat is scoredTechnique and the words to say
2Explain the purpose to the patient "I am going to use this microscope to look closely at the surface and the front structures of your eye. It uses a bright light but it does not touch your eye. Please put your chin on the chin rest and your forehead against the bar for me."
3Properly examine the entire upper and lower lids and lashes Low magnification (10×), wide diffuse beam, moderate illumination. Scan the full horizontal extent of the upper lid from medial to lateral canthus, then the lower lid. Do not stop at the centre: "entire" means the whole lid.
4Accurately describe the lids and lashes "The upper and lower lid skin is intact with no lesions, oedema, or erythema. The lashes are of normal number, direction, and distribution, with no collarettes, no scurf, no madarosis, and no trichiasis or distichiasis."
5Properly examine the entire upper and lower lid margins and the lower puncta Increase magnification to 16×. Sweep the full length of both margins. Have the patient look up and slightly out, and pull the lower lid down gently to bring the lower punctum into view. The punctum is a named item: it must be seen and described.
6Accurately describe the margins and the lower puncta "The upper and lower lid margins are smooth and regular with no notching, telangiectasia, or thickening. The meibomian gland orifices are patent and non-capped along both margins. The lower punctum is patent, of normal size, and correctly apposed to the globe."
7Properly examine the entire inferior palpebral conjunctiva using lower lid eversion Ask the patient to look up. Place your thumb or finger below the lash line and evert the lower lid to expose the full tarsal and forniceal conjunctiva, medial to lateral.
8Accurately describe the inferior palpebral conjunctiva "The inferior palpebral conjunctiva is pink and smooth, with no follicles, no papillae, no concretions, no membranes, and no scarring or symblepharon. The inferior fornix is of normal depth."
9Properly examine the entire superior palpebral conjunctiva using upper lid eversion This is the item candidates most often lose. Instruct clearly, then evert properly. "I am going to turn your upper eyelid out for a moment to look underneath it. It will feel odd but it should not hurt. Please look all the way down and keep looking down." Grasp the lashes and the lid margin, pull the lid down and away from the globe, then use a cotton-tipped applicator placed at the upper lid crease to rotate the tarsus outward. You are explicitly permitted to use a cotton-tipped applicator. Hold the eversion long enough to sweep medial to lateral. Then release gently and ask the patient to look up and blink.
10Accurately describe the superior palpebral conjunctiva "The superior palpebral conjunctiva is pink and smooth with no follicles, no papillae greater than 0.3 millimetres, no giant papillae, no concretions, no membranes, no scarring or Arlt line, and no foreign body in the subtarsal sulcus."
11Properly examine the entire bulbar conjunctiva and sclera Diffuse beam, moderate magnification. Direct the patient into all four gaze positions to expose the full bulbar surface: "Please look up for me. Now down. Now toward your nose. Now toward your ear." Include the limbus circumferentially.
12Accurately describe the bulbar conjunctiva and sclera "The bulbar conjunctiva is clear and quiet, with no injection, no chemosis, no follicles, no haemorrhage, and no pinguecula or pterygium. The limbus is intact with clear palisades of Vogt. The underlying sclera is white with no thinning, no nodules, no episcleral or deep vascular injection, and no pigmentation."
13Properly examine the entire cornea using a parallelepiped Beam width about 2 to 3 mm, angle 45 degrees, magnification 16×. Sweep the parallelepiped systematically across the entire cornea, limbus to limbus, in overlapping vertical or horizontal passes. Announce that you are doing so: "I am now examining the entire cornea with a parallelepiped, sweeping from limbus to limbus."
14Properly examine the entire cornea using an optic section Narrow the beam to the thinnest slit and increase the angle. Sweep the entire cornea again, this time announcing the layers: "I am now using an optic section to assess corneal depth and to localise any findings by layer, again sweeping the entire cornea." Both item 13 and item 14 require the entire cornea, so you sweep twice.
15Accurately describe the cornea by layer: epithelium, stroma, endothelium Describe all three layers explicitly. "The epithelium is intact, smooth, and clear with no punctate staining, no defect, and no oedema. The stroma is clear with no infiltrate, no scar, no neovascularisation, no thinning, and no striae. Descemet membrane and the endothelium appear clear with no guttae, no keratic precipitates, and no pigment deposition."
16Properly assess the temporal anterior chamber angle using the Van Herick technique Narrow parallelepiped or optic section, beam at 60 degrees from the observation axis, placed at the temporal limbus, magnification 16×. Announce it: "I am now performing a Van Herick assessment at the temporal limbus, comparing the depth of the aqueous shadow with the corneal section thickness."
17Accurately describe the temporal Van Herick finding Use the grading explicitly. "The shadow depth is greater than one half of the corneal section thickness, which is a Van Herick grade 4, a wide open angle temporally." The grades: 4 shadow greater than 1/2 corneal thickness; 3 shadow between 1/4 and 1/2; 2 shadow 1/4; 1 shadow less than 1/4; grades 1 and 2 are potentially occludable.
18Properly examine the anterior chamber for cell and flare Small, bright, conical beam (about 1 mm square or a small round aperture), maximum illumination, high magnification, beam angled at about 45 to 60 degrees, focused in the aqueous midway between the corneal endothelium and the anterior lens surface, in a darkened room. Announce it: "I am darkening the room and using a small, bright conical beam focused in the aqueous between the cornea and the lens to assess for cells and flare."
19Accurately describe cell and flare Use SUN grading. "There are no cells and no flare in the anterior chamber: grade zero cells and grade zero flare, with an optically clear aqueous." If present, grade it: 0.5+ is 1 to 5 cells, 1+ is 6 to 15, 2+ is 16 to 25, 3+ is 26 to 50, 4+ is over 50 cells in a 1 by 1 mm field.
20Properly examine the entire iris Return to a wider beam and moderate illumination. Sweep the full iris surface, 360 degrees, from the pupil margin to the root, in overlapping passes. Move the patient's gaze to see the far periphery.
21Accurately describe the iris "The iris is flat and of normal architecture and colour, with an intact collarette and normal crypts and contraction furrows. There are no naevi, no Lisch nodules, no Koeppe or Busacca nodules, no neovascularisation at the pupil margin, no transillumination defects, no atrophy, and no synechiae. The pupil is round, centred, and of normal size, with an intact pupillary ruff."
22Properly perform transillumination of the entire iris This is a distinct technique, not the same as item 20. Place a small, bright beam directly through the pupil, coaxially, to retro-illuminate the iris from behind using the fundus reflex, and move the beam across the pupil to transilluminate the whole iris, 360 degrees. Announce it: "I am now transilluminating the entire iris by directing the beam through the pupil to retro-illuminate the iris and look for transillumination defects."
23Accurately describe iris transillumination "On transillumination the entire iris is intact with no transillumination defects: no mid-peripheral radial spoke-like defects and no moth-eaten peripupillary defects."
24Examine the ocular tissues in a smooth, logical sequence Move front to back and outside in: lids and lashes, margins and puncta, palpebral conjunctiva (lower then upper), bulbar conjunctiva and sclera, cornea (parallelepiped then optic section), Van Herick, anterior chamber, iris, iris transillumination. Do not jump back and forth, do not re-do structures, and do not change magnification and illumination more than necessary.
25Properly instruct the patient throughout Give a clear instruction before every change: "Look up for me. Now down. Look at my ear. I am going to turn your lid out now, please keep looking down. I am going to make the light brighter, this may feel uncomfortable but it will only be a moment. You can blink normally whenever you need to." Warn before every bright light and before every touch.

Skill 2 · Four-mirror gonioscopy (items 26 to 41)

Non-negotiables for this skill
  • State that you would disinfect the lens even though you assume it is clean.
  • Anaesthetic in the examined eye only.
  • A cushioning solution such as Celluvisc must be used on the lens for patient comfort. This is explicitly required by the candidate guide.
  • All four quadrants, each with a clear, centred, maintained view of the entire quadrant, and each correctly named.
  • Identify the most posterior visible angle structure in every quadrant, and the iris approach in the inferior quadrant (item 31 asks for quadrant, most posterior structure, and iris approach).
  • Maintain control of the lens at all times (item 38): never let go, never let it slide uncontrolled, keep a stable brace on the patient's cheek or the slit lamp.
  • Assess and describe corneal integrity without staining at the conclusion (item 40): use the white light and a parallelepiped, not fluorescein.
Item-by-item script and technique
ItemWhat is scoredTechnique and the words to say
26Explain the purpose, including the drop "Next I am going to look at the drainage angle inside your eye, which is where fluid leaves the eye. To do that I need to place a small lens gently on the front of your eye. First I will put in an anaesthetic drop so that it is comfortable and you will not feel the lens. The drop may sting for a second or two." Naming the drop is part of the item.
27Properly prepare the lens and instil anaesthetic into the appropriate eye State the disinfection intention, then apply the cushioning solution to the lens face: "I would disinfect the lens prior to use. I am applying a cushioning solution to the lens surface for your comfort." Instil proparacaine 0.5% into the examined eye only, without touching the lids or lashes with the bottle, and confirm which eye out loud.
28Provide proper instructions to the patient "Please put your chin on the rest and your forehead forward. I want you to keep both eyes open and look straight ahead at the light in front of you. Keep breathing normally, try not to squeeze, and tell me if you are uncomfortable at any point."
29Insert and use the lens safely and efficiently Low magnification, short vertical beam, moderate illumination, room lights low. Approach from below or from the side, brace your hand against the patient's cheek or the headrest, and place the lens gently and squarely on the cornea without pressing. Avoid the lashes. Do not indent unless you intend to.
30Clear, maintained image of the entire inferior quadrant The superior mirror views the inferior angle. Centre the view, keep the beam perpendicular to the mirror, and sweep the full extent of the quadrant. Hold it: a fleeting view does not count. Ask if needed: "Do you have a view?"
31Accurately identify the inferior quadrant, the most posterior visible structure, and the iris approach "I am viewing the inferior angle through the superior mirror. The most posterior structure visible is the ciliary body band. The iris approach is regular and flat with a normal insertion, and the angle is wide open, graded Shaffer 4. There are no peripheral anterior synechiae, no neovascularisation, and trabecular pigmentation is grade 1."
32–33Nasal quadrant: view and identification Rotate the lens 90 degrees. The temporal mirror views the nasal angle. "I am now viewing the nasal angle. The most posterior visible structure is the ciliary body band, the angle is open, Shaffer grade 4, with grade 1 pigmentation and no synechiae."
34–35Superior quadrant: view and identification The inferior mirror views the superior angle. The superior angle is normally the narrowest, so expect a different answer. "I am now viewing the superior angle. The most posterior visible structure is the anterior trabecular meshwork, the angle is narrower here, Shaffer grade 2 to 3, with grade 2 pigmentation and no synechiae."
36–37Temporal quadrant: view and identification The nasal mirror views the temporal angle. "I am now viewing the temporal angle. The most posterior visible structure is the ciliary body band, Shaffer grade 4, grade 1 pigmentation, no synechiae."
38Maintain control of the lens at all times Keep three points of contact: fingers on the lens, hand braced on the cheek or headrest, and the patient's head stable against the bar. Never release the lens while it is on the eye, and never let it slide across the cornea uncontrolled.
39Remove the lens safely and efficiently Warn the patient, then lift the lens straight off without dragging. "I am taking the lens off now, please keep looking straight ahead." Do not ask the patient to blink or squeeze it off.
40Assess and describe corneal integrity without staining Return to the slit lamp, white light, parallelepiped, and re-examine the cornea. "Following lens removal I am reassessing corneal integrity without stain. The epithelium is intact and smooth with no abrasion, no epithelial disruption, and no oedema." Do not use fluorescein for this item.
41Smooth and logical sequence Explain, anaesthetise, prepare, instruct, insert, then go around the angle in one continuous rotation (for example inferior, nasal, superior, temporal), remove, then reassess the cornea. Do not revisit quadrants.
Mirror to quadrant, and the two grading systems

The mirror is opposite the angle you see. Superior mirror shows the inferior angle; inferior mirror shows the superior angle; nasal mirror shows the temporal angle; temporal mirror shows the nasal angle.

Angle structures, anterior to posterior: Schwalbe line, trabecular meshwork, scleral spur, ciliary body band, iris root.

Shaffer grading (angular width): grade 4 is 35 to 45 degrees and wide open; grade 3 is 20 to 35 degrees; grade 2 is 20 degrees and narrow; grade 1 is 10 degrees and extremely narrow; grade 0 is closed. Spaeth additionally records iris insertion, angular width, and iris configuration.

Skill 3 · Goldmann applanation tonometry (items 42 to 58)

Item-by-item script and technique
ItemWhat is scoredTechnique and the words to say
42Explain the purpose, including the drop "Now I am going to measure the pressure inside your eye. I will put in a drop that has an anaesthetic and a yellow dye in it, so the eye is numb and I can see the measurement. The dye may make your vision look a little yellow for a few minutes. A small blue-lit probe will touch the front of your eye very gently; you should not feel it."
43Instil the anaesthetic and fluorescein combination properly in one eye State the disinfection intention for the probe first. Then instil the combination drop (benoxinate with fluorescein, or proparacaine plus a fluorescein strip) into the examined eye only, without touching the eye or lashes. Confirm the eye out loud. Ask the patient to blink a few times to distribute the dye.
44Adjust the illumination system properly Cobalt blue filter, widest beam, full illumination, illumination arm swung about 60 degrees temporally from the observation axis so the beam floods the prism. Magnification low (10×). Say what you are doing: "I am setting the cobalt blue filter, the widest beam at full illumination, with the illumination arm at approximately 60 degrees."
45Assess and describe the cornea for staining before tonometry Look with the blue light before you touch the eye. "Before tonometry I am assessing the cornea with the cobalt blue filter. There is no punctate staining, no epithelial defect, and no abrasion; the epithelium is intact." This is a separate scored item from item 56, which is the post-tonometry assessment.
46Assess and describe the quality of the tear film before tonometry "The tear film is of adequate volume with a normal meniscus height and no excess fluorescein pooling, no debris, and a smooth even distribution." Excess fluorescein widens the mires and overestimates pressure; too little narrows them and underestimates it, so this assessment has a direct measurement purpose.
47Set the probe pressure at an appropriate level Set the dial to about 1, meaning 10 mmHg, before contact. "I am pre-setting the dial to 10 millimetres of mercury." Starting at zero risks a sudden jump; starting very high risks corneal indentation.
48Properly position the tonometer and align the probe Swing the tonometer into place with the prism set at 0 degrees on the graduation (valid for under three dioptres of corneal cylinder, which you are told to assume). Align the prism centre to the corneal apex, at the same height as the patient's pupil, and centre it in the beam.
49Provide proper instructions, including a fixation target Naming the fixation target is part of the item. "Please keep both eyes wide open and look straight ahead past my ear at the corner of the wall. Try not to squeeze or hold your breath. Keep looking at that spot and breathe normally. It will touch very gently and you should not feel it."
50Applanate safely and efficiently Watch from the side, outside the oculars, as you advance until the prism just touches, then look through the left ocular. Advance smoothly, do not push, and do not contact the lids or lashes. Retract at once if the patient squeezes.
51Adjust so the semicircles are symmetrical and centred Use the joystick to centre the mires horizontally and vertically so the two semicircles are of equal size and sit in the middle of the field. Say it: "I am centring the mires and equalising the two semicircles."
52Ensure the mires are of appropriate width Mire width should be about one tenth of the diameter of the applanated area. If they are too thick, there is too much fluorescein or tears: dry the prism and re-instil less. If too thin, add fluorescein. Say what you are judging: "The mire width is appropriate, approximately one tenth of the ring diameter."
53Properly align the mires Turn the dial until the inner edges of the two semicircles just touch, forming an even figure of eight, and take the reading at the midpoint of the pulsation. Overlapping means the reading is too low; separated means too high.
54Safely retract the tonometer Withdraw the joystick smoothly and swing the arm away before the patient moves. "I am moving the instrument away now, you can blink."
55Accurately state the obtained finding with the time The time is part of the item. "The intraocular pressure of the right eye by Goldmann applanation is 16 millimetres of mercury at 10:42 in the morning." State the eye, the method, the value, and the clock time.
56Assess and describe the cornea using the blue filter after tonometry Re-examine with the cobalt blue filter. "Following tonometry I am reassessing the cornea with the cobalt blue filter. The epithelium remains intact with no punctate staining and no abrasion from the probe."
57Smooth and logical sequence Explain, disinfect statement, instil, set illumination, assess cornea, assess tear film, set the dial, position and align, instruct with a fixation target, applanate, centre and equalise, check width, align mires, read, retract, state with time, reassess the cornea, then educate.
58Educate and explain the patient's IOP A separate scored item, and easy to forget in the rush to finish. "Your eye pressure today was 16, which is in the normal range. Eye pressure matters because pressure that is too high over time can damage the nerve at the back of the eye, which is what happens in glaucoma. Yours is healthy, and we will check it again at each visit so we can see any change over time."
The ten items most commonly lost in this station
  1. Item 1: forgetting to say you would disinfect the instruments.
  2. Item 9: an inadequate or absent upper lid eversion. Practise it until it takes eight seconds.
  3. Items 5 and 6: omitting the lower punctum.
  4. Item 14: doing an optic section only centrally instead of sweeping the entire cornea.
  5. Items 16 and 17: forgetting the Van Herick assessment entirely, or not stating a grade.
  6. Items 22 and 23: treating iris transillumination as the same as examining the iris. It is a separate technique and a separate pair of items.
  7. Item 31: giving the quadrant and the posterior structure but forgetting the iris approach.
  8. Item 40: staining the cornea to check integrity, when the item specifies without staining.
  9. Item 55: stating the pressure without the time.
  10. Item 58: finishing the measurement and never explaining the pressure to the patient.
A fifteen-minute pacing plan for the anterior station
ClockTask
0:00–0:30Introduce, ask which eye, state the disinfection intention, position the patient.
0:30–6:00Biomicroscopy. Lids and lashes, margins and puncta, lower then upper palpebral conjunctiva, bulbar conjunctiva and sclera, cornea by parallelepiped then optic section, Van Herick, cells and flare, iris, iris transillumination.
6:00–10:30Gonioscopy. Explain and anaesthetise, prepare with cushioning solution, insert, four quadrants in one rotation, remove, reassess cornea without stain.
10:30–14:30Applanation. Explain and instil, set illumination, pre-tonometry cornea and tear film, set dial, align, instruct with fixation target, applanate, read, retract, state with time, post-tonometry cornea, educate the pressure.
14:30–15:00Buffer. If you are ahead, repeat any item you are unsure of, announcing it. Remain in the room until the announcement.

If you are behind at 10:30, move to tonometry anyway. Tonometry contains 17 items, and finishing it is worth more than perfecting a gonioscopy quadrant.

Key
  1. Stating that you would properly disinfect the ocular instruments prior to using them. Say it in your opening, explicitly, once.
  2. Items 13 and 14. Item 13 requires a parallelepiped swept over the entire cornea; item 14 requires an optic section swept over the entire cornea. You sweep twice.
  3. Grade 4: shadow greater than half the corneal section thickness. Grade 3: between a quarter and a half. Grade 2: a quarter. Grade 1: less than a quarter. Grades 1 and 2 are potentially occludable.
  4. A small, bright conical beam at maximum illumination, high magnification, angled about 45 to 60 degrees, focused in the aqueous midway between the corneal endothelium and the anterior lens surface, in a darkened room.
  5. Examining the iris uses a wider beam on the iris surface; transillumination directs the beam through the pupil to retro-illuminate the iris using the fundus reflex, moving the beam across the pupil to cover the entire iris and reveal transillumination defects.
  6. Apply a cushioning solution such as Celluvisc, and instil anaesthetic in the examined eye only. You must state that you would disinfect the lens prior to use.
  7. The mirror is opposite the quadrant viewed: superior mirror shows the inferior angle, inferior mirror the superior angle, nasal mirror the temporal angle, and temporal mirror the nasal angle.
  8. The inferior quadrant, item 31: quadrant, most posterior visible structure, and iris approach.
  9. With white light and a parallelepiped at the slit lamp, describing epithelial integrity. Do not use fluorescein: the item specifies without staining.
  10. Cobalt blue filter, widest beam, full illumination, illumination arm at about 60 degrees, low magnification.
  11. Corneal staining (item 45) and tear film quality (item 46). Excess fluorescein widens the mires and overestimates pressure; too little narrows them and underestimates it, and pre-existing staining must be distinguished from probe-induced staining afterwards.
  12. Width about one tenth of the diameter of the applanated ring; the endpoint is when the inner edges of the two semicircles just touch, read at the midpoint of the pulsation.
  13. The eye, the method, and the time: "IOP right eye by Goldmann applanation is 16 mmHg at 10:42."
  14. Educating and explaining the patient's IOP. It is lost because candidates treat the reading as the end of the skill and move on.
  15. Move to tonometry. Tonometry has 17 items and gonioscopy quadrants are worth fewer; completing the remaining skill earns more than perfecting one quadrant.
Chapter recap
  1. Say you would disinfect, ask which eye, and narrate everything.
  2. Half the items are "accurately describe": they are earned only by speaking, in medical record language.
  3. "Entire" means 360 degrees; a fleeting view scores nothing; the left ocular is what is recorded.
  4. Upper lid eversion, the lower punctum, Van Herick, and iris transillumination are the four most missed biomicroscopy elements.
  5. Sweep the whole cornea twice: parallelepiped, then optic section.
  6. Gonioscopy needs a cushioning solution, four centred maintained quadrant views, and the iris approach inferiorly.
  7. Reassess the cornea after gonioscopy without stain.
  8. Applanation: blue filter, widest beam, 60 degrees, dial at 10, fixation target named, mires one tenth wide and inner edges touching.
  9. State the pressure with the eye, the method, and the clock time.
  10. Finish by explaining the pressure to the patient. That is a scored item.
