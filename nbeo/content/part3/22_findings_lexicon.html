Chapter 7Findings lexicon: the exact words to say

Half of the skills items are "accurately describe" Never say WNL, fine, OK, or not bad
The rule this chapter exists to enforce

The candidate guide is explicit: state your findings in the same manner as you would enter them into a patient's medical record, and avoid terminology such as "OK", "fine", "WNL", or "not bad". Every "accurately describe" item is earned by naming the structure and then describing its state in clinical terms.

The pattern to internalise for every structure is: name the structure, state that you are examining it, then give a positive description plus the pertinent negatives. Positive description first, negatives second, and never a bare "normal".

Anterior segment lexicon

Structure by structure, with a normal script and the abnormal vocabulary
StructureNormal scriptAbnormal vocabulary you may need
Lids and lashes "Lid skin intact, no lesions, oedema, or erythema. Lashes normal in number, direction, and distribution." Erythema, oedema, ecchymosis, laceration, chalazion, hordeolum, papilloma, seborrhoeic keratosis, basal cell carcinoma with a pearly rolled edge, ptosis with MRD1 measurement, dermatochalasis, ectropion, entropion, lagophthalmos, madarosis, poliosis, trichiasis, distichiasis, collarettes (cylindrical dandruff), scurf, crusting.
Lid margins "Margins smooth and regular, no notching, telangiectasia, or thickening. Meibomian orifices patent and non-capped." Capped or plugged orifices, pouting, telangiectasia, thickening, keratinisation, notching, posterior displacement of the mucocutaneous junction, turbid or toothpaste-like expression, gland dropout, foamy tear film, anterior or posterior blepharitis.
Puncta "Lower punctum patent, normal size, correctly apposed to the globe." Stenotic, occluded, everted, agenesis, punctal plug in situ, pouting with expressible concretions (canaliculitis), supernumerary punctum.
Palpebral conjunctiva "Pink and smooth, no follicles, papillae, concretions, membranes, or scarring. Fornix of normal depth." Follicles (avascular with encircling vessels), papillae with a central vessel, giant papillae over 0.3 mm, membrane, pseudomembrane, concretions, symblepharon, fornix foreshortening, Arlt line, subtarsal foreign body, chemosis, injection graded 1 to 4.
Bulbar conjunctiva and sclera "Conjunctiva clear and quiet, no injection or chemosis. Limbus intact with clear palisades of Vogt. Sclera white, no thinning or nodules." Diffuse or sectoral injection, ciliary flush, chemosis, subconjunctival haemorrhage, pinguecula, pterygium with a Stocker line, follicles, papillae, conjunctivochalasis, pigmented lesion with or without intralesional cysts, primary acquired melanosis, salmon-pink infiltrate, episcleral versus deep vascular injection, scleral nodule, scleral thinning with uveal show, staphyloma.
Cornea, epithelium "Epithelium intact, smooth, and clear, no punctate staining, defect, or oedema." Superficial punctate keratitis, graded and located; epithelial defect with measured dimensions; dendrite with terminal bulbs; pseudodendrite; microcystic oedema; bullae; map-dot-fingerprint changes; Salzmann nodules; iron line (Hudson-Stähli, Fleischer, Stocker, Ferry); verticillata; recurrent erosion.
Cornea, stroma "Stroma clear, no infiltrate, scar, neovascularisation, thinning, or striae." Infiltrate with measured size, depth, location, and margin character (feathery, ring, satellite); scar; oedema with striae or folds; thinning with a measured percentage; neovascularisation, superficial or deep, with the clock hours; Vogt striae; lipid deposition; crystalline deposits; Wessely immune ring; Descemet folds.
Cornea, endothelium and Descemet "Descemet membrane and endothelium clear, no guttae, keratic precipitates, or pigment." Central guttae with a beaten-metal appearance; peripheral Hassall-Henle bodies; keratic precipitates, fine and non-pigmented or large and mutton-fat, in an Arlt triangle; Krukenberg spindle; pigment dusting; Khodadoust line; break in Descemet with hydrops; Kayser-Fleischer ring.
Anterior chamber depth (Van Herick) "Temporal shadow depth greater than one half of the corneal section thickness: Van Herick grade 4, wide open." Grade 3 (between a quarter and a half), grade 2 (one quarter), grade 1 (less than one quarter, occludable); shallow peripherally with a convex iris; irregularly shallow.
Anterior chamber contents "Grade zero cells, grade zero flare, optically clear aqueous." Cells graded 0.5+ through 4+ by SUN; flare graded 1+ through 4+; fibrin; hypopyon with a measured height; hyphaema graded by the fraction filled; pigment; white blood cells versus red; plasmoid aqueous.
Iris "Iris flat, normal architecture and colour, intact collarette, no naevi, nodules, neovascularisation, atrophy, or synechiae. Pupil round, central, normal size, with an intact pupillary ruff." Naevus, Lisch nodules, Koeppe and Busacca nodules, neovascularisation at the pupil margin or in the angle, sectoral atrophy, diffuse stromal atrophy with heterochromia, iridodialysis, sphincter tears, corectopia, polycoria, transillumination defects (mid-peripheral radial or peripupillary moth-eaten), posterior or anterior synechiae, iris bombé, plateau configuration, persistent pupillary membrane.
Iris transillumination "On transillumination the entire iris is intact with no transillumination defects." Mid-peripheral radial spoke-like defects (pigment dispersion), peripupillary moth-eaten defects (pseudoexfoliation), sectoral defects (post-herpetic or post-iridotomy), full-thickness defect from trauma or a laser iridotomy.
Crystalline lens "Lens clear throughout all layers, clear anterior and posterior capsule, no nuclear sclerosis, cortical spoking, or posterior subcapsular change." Nuclear sclerosis graded 1 to 4; cortical spoking with location; posterior subcapsular plaque; anterior subcapsular; polar opacity; vacuoles; pseudoexfoliative material in a target pattern; phacodonesis; subluxation with the direction; Vossius ring; rosette cataract; Christmas tree; oil droplet; sunflower; Mittendorf dot; posterior capsular opacification; Elschnig pearls; an intraocular lens with its position and centration.
Anterior vitreous "Retrolental space and anterior vitreous optically clear, no cells, pigment, haemorrhage, or asteroid hyalosis." Cells graded, pigment granules (tobacco dust or Shafer sign), red blood cells, asteroid hyalosis, anterior vitreous strands, vitreous in the anterior chamber, syneretic cavities.

Dot-blot haemorrhage; flame-shaped haemorrhage; microaneurysm; cotton wool spot; hard exudate; Roth spot; retinal whitening; commotio; choroidal rupture; drusen; chorioretinal atrophy; myelinated nerve fibres; naevus with measured dimensions; laser scars.

Phrases to use, and phrases never to use

Language discipline
Never saySay instead
"Lids are fine.""Lid skin is intact with no lesions, oedema, or erythema, and the lashes are normal in number, direction, and distribution."
"Cornea WNL.""The epithelium is intact and clear with no staining, the stroma is clear with no infiltrate or scar, and the endothelium is clear with no guttae or precipitates."
"Anterior chamber deep and quiet.""Van Herick grade 4 temporally, and grade zero cells with grade zero flare in an optically clear aqueous."
"Angle open.""Inferior angle viewed through the superior mirror: the most posterior visible structure is the ciliary body band, the iris approach is regular and flat, Shaffer grade 4, trabecular pigmentation grade 1, no synechiae."
"Pressure 16.""The intraocular pressure of the right eye by Goldmann applanation is 16 millimetres of mercury at 10:42."
"Disc looks good.""The optic nerve head is pink with no pallor, the rim is intact and follows the ISNT rule, the contour is regular with no undermining, there is no peripapillary atrophy and no disc haemorrhage. Horizontal cup-to-disc 0.3, vertical cup-to-disc 0.3."
"Macula OK.""The macula is flat and intact with even pigmentation and no drusen, mottling, haemorrhage, exudate, or oedema. The fovea is flat with a crisp foveal reflex."
"Periphery clear.""The superior-temporal peripheral retina from the equator to the ora serrata is flat and intact with a vortex vein ampulla in view, and there are no holes, tears, or lattice degeneration."
"Not bad."Nothing. Describe it.
"Everything looks normal."Name each structure and describe it individually. A global statement earns no individual items.
Two habits that generate the language automatically
  1. Announce, then describe. "I am now examining X" followed by "X is ..." creates the two-part structure the form rewards, because most structures generate a properly examine item and an accurately describe item.
  2. Two positives and three negatives. For every structure, give two positive descriptors (colour, contour, clarity, calibre, integrity) and three pertinent negatives (the abnormalities that would matter most for that structure). That formula produces a record-quality description in under ten seconds and prevents the reflex to say "normal".

Chapter recap
  1. Half the skills items are description items, earned only by speaking in record language.
  2. Never say WNL, fine, OK, or not bad. Describe.
  3. Announce the structure, then describe it: that mirrors the two-item structure of the form.
  4. Two positive descriptors plus three pertinent negatives, every time.
  5. Grade what can be graded: Van Herick, SUN cells and flare, Shaffer, pigmentation, cup ratios.
  6. State the pressure with eye, method, and time.
  7. Name the quadrant, the most posterior angle structure, and the iris approach in gonioscopy.
  8. Give the optic nerve four features and two separate cup ratios.
  9. Describe the fovea separately from the macula.
  10. Name the vortex vein ampulla in every peripheral view.
