# Web research

Investigate before designing anything.

## Go wide before synthesizing

- Run multiple searches: four to ten, never fewer than four. One search bets everything on your first phrasing. Stop when new queries stop surfacing new information, even if that takes more than ten.
- Vary the queries. Split the ask into concrete sub-questions, and hit the important ones from several angles with different terms and different source types.
- Fetch a lot, not just the top hit. Favor the primary source behind a hit - the paper, the filing, the announcement, the docs - over a blog's summary of it, and extract specifics: numbers, dates, names, direct quotes.
- Cross-check every load-bearing figure across independent sources. When sources disagree, report the disagreement rather than averaging it away or silently picking one.
- Keep the trail as you go: which source said what, with URLs.

## Epistemics in the deliverable

- Attribute every substantive claim inline, linked to its source.
- Date what you cite ("as of the 2024 filing"). Stale numbers presented as current are worse than no numbers.
- Separate what sources establish from what you infer, and say which is which. Where evidence is thin or conflicting, the report says so. A confident-sounding gap is the failure mode to avoid.
- Quote sparingly: short quotes in quotation marks, no reproduction of long passages or creative work, no multi-paragraph summary of a single source.

## Deliverable

Unless LO asks for another format: a designed single-file HTML research report. Headline takeaways up top, then findings with their evidence, then a linked source list at the end. Design it like an editorial broadsheet - strong typographic hierarchy, pull quotes for the key numbers, charts only where the data earns them.
